# Little Fixers Club — Scout's Checklist Acting (PR #32)

This is a deliberately **separate 60-second original cartoon**, not another poster-based slideshow and **not a completed 20-minute episode**. It builds on the 34-second full-body, voiced Scout performance from PR #31 and adds 26 seconds of genuine puppet/object acting using the existing ChipToons interaction renderer.

## How to open

On the no-login home screen choose **Watch Scout pick up a checklist (1:00)**. The earlier **Try the full-body cartoon workshop** and **Watch Scout walk and talk** options remain intact.

## What happens on screen

- **00:00–00:34:** The previously approved, 34-second vector Scout voice-and-walk sequence (three original Little Fixers recordings, waveform mouth movement, timed captions). Its scene, voice approvals and recorded assets remain unchanged.
- **00:34–00:38:** Scout walks toward the bench on alternating legs, notices the picture checklist and looks curious.
- **00:38–00:45:** Scout reaches to pick up a **real story-object book prop**; the object becomes attached to Scout's animated right-hand position rather than simply appearing in a second still drawing. Scout lifts and displays it.
- **00:45–00:50:** Scout lowers and **releases the book** onto the work surface. It becomes stationary afterward.
- **00:51–01:00:** Scout pauses thoughtfully, points at the plan and smiles; the lesson reminds children that a trusted grown-up must inspect dangerous tools and unstable furniture.

The new performance adds **14 explicit authored keyframes** for full-body gesture and facial emotion, three distinct camera shots, four teaching labels, one object pick-up/put-down interaction, one pointing interaction, timed sound effects and subtle workshop depth.

## Honest voice and media status

The original **34 seconds remain voiced and approved**. The new 26-second scene includes **two written but UNRECORDED lines**. No fake dialogue, synthetic speech, generated mouth track or timed subtitles are added for those lines. The new scene's four effects clips include existing ambience/success, plus small original paper and placement cues. Audio sources stay well within the project limit.

This is SVG-based **2.5D animated vector art**, not a true 3D-mesh film. It is a prototype that demonstrates an expressive character handling a real object, a concrete step toward production-quality cartoons.
