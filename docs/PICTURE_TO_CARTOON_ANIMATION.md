# Picture-to-Cartoon Animation Studio

## Start with a picture

1. From **My Cartoons**, choose **Turn My Picture Into a Cartoon**. Upload your own PNG, JPEG, or WebP, or open the supplied **Little Fixers Club — Fix It, Don’t Toss It!** poster. **Download original picture** returns the original bytes.
2. Drag a rectangle at fit zoom or enter crop measurements with the keyboard. Name the panel and choose whether it is a reference or a visible background. Create a scene, then use **Picture & cutouts** in the editor to select another panel for any storyboard scene.
3. Choose **Make This Character Move**. Tap around the artwork to close an outline or enter individual point coordinates. Zoom in for edges. **Remove area** erases inside a second outline. **Add or restore area** restores artwork. Undo a closed outline to adjust its points. Preview the transparent result before saving it as a prop or keeping it as a body part.
4. Prepare a body, head, arms, legs, and any other parts the pose needs. Choose **Continue in Puppet Maker** to review the joints and pivots. Use the Drawing Pad or upload separate artwork for missing parts and alternate eye and mouth images.
5. Choose **Add to Cartoon** to save the puppet in the Art Library and place it in the scene. Reuse it from **Characters** in other scenes. The project shares identical artwork across placements and stores each scene’s poses and timing separately.
6. Use the existing animation timeline, Scene & World Builder, Camera & Shot Director, Script & Voice Director, and sound tools. **Finish My Cartoon** renders a playable WebM with your chosen captions and audio. Save a **Project backup** to take the cartoon and its source picture to another browser. Back up your Art Library separately to keep unused library entries.

## Little Fixers Club example

**Open the animated workshop demo** makes a new, editable 34-second cartoon from the supplied poster. The workshop host has separate head, arm, eye, and mouth artwork. She gestures and reacts as the chair wobbles. The camera moves in to show where the wooden pieces meet, then the chair is shown set aside with a reminder to wait for an adult. The demo includes synthetic dialogue, timed caption phrases, and a workshop sound.

The separate **Open the editable 20-minute plan** option creates a storyboard with suggested panels and lesson beats. It is a starting plan for a longer episode.

## Artwork limits

Trace the visible parts and supply missing pieces manually. Artwork behind other objects, alternate views, and moving facial parts need separate drawings or uploads. Text and characters printed in a scene background stay still. The poster is preserved once per project, while transparent cutouts and character parts are separate, editable artwork.

## Verification

The browser tests cover original bytes, panel settings, keyboard cutouts, erase and restore, library saves, cross-scene character reuse, and project backup and restore. They run on desktop, phone, and tablet viewports. The media test exports the complete short, checks audible Opus, decodes the WebM independently, and measures head, arm, and chair movement in separate regions of the picture. The existing 20-minute production proof remains part of Studio checks.
