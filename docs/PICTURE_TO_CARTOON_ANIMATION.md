# Picture-to-Cartoon Animation Studio

## Start with a picture

1. From **My Cartoons**, choose **Turn My Picture Into a Cartoon**. Upload your own PNG, JPEG, or WebP, or open the supplied **Little Fixers Club — Fix It, Don’t Toss It!** poster. **Download original picture** returns the original bytes.
2. Drag a rectangle at fit zoom or enter crop measurements with the keyboard. Name the panel and choose whether it is a reference or a visible background. Create a scene, then use **Picture & cutouts** in the editor to select another panel for any storyboard scene.
3. Choose **Make This Character Move**. Tap around the artwork to close an outline or enter individual point coordinates. Zoom in for edges. **Remove area** erases inside a second outline. **Add or restore area** restores artwork. Undo a closed outline to adjust its points. For a simple, nearly flat background inside the outline, turn on **Clean up a plain background inside this cutout**, pick a visible background spot, and start with Low strength. Inspect the preview and lower the strength or adjust the outline if the subject is removed. You can also choose **Original picture colors**, **Soft 2D storybook colors**, or **Bold 2D ink and colors**. Preview again after changing an option before saving the prop or body part.
4. Prepare a body, head, upper arms, forearms, hands, upper legs, shins, feet, and any other parts the pose needs. The traced forearms attach to matching arms, hands to forearms (or arms), shins to legs and feet to shins (or legs). The unposed cutout centers stay in their original pictured positions while their joints start near the ends of the parts. If a parent is absent, the piece attaches to the body; reconnect it after adding the missing part in Puppet Maker. Changing its parent keeps the still art in place, but changes how saved motion moves that part, so review poses and preview the action. Choose **Continue in Puppet Maker** to adjust joints and pivots. For a cutout of one arm, forearm, hand, leg, shin or foot, select that part and choose **Mirror artwork to [other limb]**. The other part gets a horizontally flipped copy of the same artwork with its own joint and position; if its parent is another limb, create the other parent first. Use **Flip this part artwork horizontally** to correct orientation. Replace the reused art with a drawing or separate upload if the sides look different, and use separate artwork for missing details or alternate eye and mouth images.
5. For an uploaded foot picture, select that part in Puppet Maker and use **Mark the shoe sole** to place the yellow dot where the visible shoe touches the ground. The two controls work on a phone; the opposite mirrored foot gets the matching reversed mark. **Review Character Feet** in the scene editor then uses that mark when it suggests frames to inspect. The mark does not edit the picture or automatically repair a slide.
6. Choose **Add to Cartoon** to save the puppet in the Art Library and place it in the scene. Reuse it from **Characters** in other scenes. The project shares identical artwork across placements and stores each scene’s poses and timing separately.
7. Use the existing animation timeline, Scene & World Builder, Camera & Shot Director, Script & Voice Director, and sound tools. **Finish My Cartoon** renders a playable WebM with your chosen captions and audio. Save a **Project backup** to take the cartoon and its source picture to another browser. Back up your Art Library separately to keep unused library entries.

## Little Fixers Club example

**Open the animated workshop demo** makes a new, editable 34-second cartoon from the supplied poster. The workshop host has separate head, arm, eye, and mouth artwork. She gestures and reacts as the chair wobbles. The camera moves in to show where the wooden pieces meet, then the chair is shown set aside with a reminder to wait for an adult. The demo includes synthetic dialogue, timed caption phrases, and a workshop sound.

The separate **Open the editable 20-minute plan** option creates a storyboard with suggested panels and lesson beats. It is a starting plan for a longer episode.

**Open Episode 1 animated opening** creates a separate 1:59 cartoon from the same poster, with a cold open, welcome, and safe-choice activity. [Episode 1 guide](LITTLE_FIXERS_EPISODE_1_OPENING.md)

## Artwork limits

The optional 2D color finishes and plain-background cleanup run locally and affect only derived transparent cutouts. Cleanup removes color connected to the picked spot within the outline; it works best with a nearly solid backdrop. It does not separate a person from busy scenery or infer hidden body parts. Color finishes simplify colors and, for the bold option, darken edges inside the mask. They do not identify a person, invent anatomy, create anime/3D geometry, or add a puppet skeleton. Mirror artwork reuses visible pixels; it cannot recover an occluded limb, its opposite view or an asymmetric costume. Trace the visible parts and supply missing details manually. Artwork behind other objects, alternate views, and moving facial parts need separate drawings or uploads. Text and characters printed in a scene background stay still. The poster is preserved once per project, while transparent cutouts and character parts are separate, editable artwork.

## Verification

The browser tests cover original bytes, panel settings, keyboard cutouts, erase and restore, library saves, cross-scene character reuse, articulated picture-part selection, mirrored-art editing, parent reconnection, and project backup and restore. They run on desktop, phone, and tablet viewports. The reparenting media test exports a waving arm and checks the painted hand's position in independently decoded WebM frames. The nested-joint media test independently decodes a moving WebM and checks painted forearm and shin pixels. The mirror media test checks the flipped Stage image and independently decodes a downloaded moving WebM with the painted arm colors. The color-finish media test checks actual derived PNG pixels and unchanged transparency, then independently decodes a moving puppet in a downloaded WebM. The plain-background test checks a genuine uploaded PNG, removed background pixels, preserved subject/source and a moving transparent puppet in an independently decoded WebM. The workshop media test exports the complete short, checks audible Opus, decodes the WebM independently, and measures head, arm, and chair movement in separate regions of the picture. The existing 20-minute production proof remains part of Studio checks.
