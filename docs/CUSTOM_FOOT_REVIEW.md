# Review a custom character's foot movement

In **Edit scene → Characters & movement**, select an animated puppet and open **Review Character Feet**. Choose left or right. The studio samples up to three seconds of that character's actual Stage motion starting near the current playhead. **Review From Current Frame** moves the inspection window; a suggested frame opens the existing Stage preview. Looking at the review alone does not rewrite the animation, source picture, project history or exported video.

The review works with separately parented vector or uploaded-picture foot parts outside the Workshop seating sequence. For each 24-fps sample, it projects the foot's sole point through its saved joints and actor transform. For an uploaded picture foot, open **Puppet Maker → Build a Character**, select the foot under **Body-part hierarchy**, assign its artwork and use **Mark the shoe sole** to move the yellow dot across and down the displayed picture. The mark is saved with that puppet; mirrored feet put it on the opposite side. **Reset sole to picture bottom** restores the default. Existing puppets without a mark still use the bottom center of their part rectangle. The original PNG, joint position and visible animation stay unchanged.

The review compares successive positions close to the rig's neutral-foot floor estimate and links up to five of the largest sideways intervals. The floor estimate moves with the character. It does not inspect transparent pixels, automatically find the real shoe sole, read scenery floor or camera depth, or detect collisions and physical balance. A mistaken mark can make the estimate inaccurate. A clean result is not proof of contact or natural motion.

The same review now groups frames where the selected sole is at least four stage pixels **below its own neutral-pose foot line** while the actor is upright. Each warning names the deepest offset and its frame range; **Show Below-Baseline Frame** opens that saved Stage pose without changing any key. Check the actual scenery and character artwork there. The line follows the actor rather than the environment, and a stylized pose may intentionally cross it. This warning does not establish that a foot clipped through a real floor.

For a flat stage floor, pause on a frame where your selected foot visibly touches it and choose **Set Scene Ground From This Foot**. The guide saves that foot's exact Stage Y height with the scene and appears as a dashed line in the paused editor preview. It is shared by all feet and characters in that scene, survives backups and Undo/Redo, and stays fixed when an actor moves vertically. **Clear Scene Ground Guide** removes it. The three-second review groups upright samples at least four pixels below this saved height and lets you open the deepest frame. The guide is never part of the exported cartoon. Choose a different grounded frame and set it again if the reference is wrong.

For a simple incline, set the first point as above, seek to a second grounded foot at least 80 Stage pixels sideways, then choose **Set Second Slope Point From This Foot**. The editor displays a line segment between those two saved soles. The review interpolates only inside that X span; outside it, it makes no below-guide claim. An excessively steep pair (more than one pixel vertically for two horizontally) is refused. Set the first point again to restart with a flat line, or set the second again to adjust the slope. Older flat-scene backups remain usable; set their first point again to give the slope an X position.

The paused editor now draws the guide inside the same camera coordinate system as the puppet. Pan, zoom and character-focused shots move the guide with the saved foot positions; play mode and exported video omit it. The review still compares saved world-space soles and ground points, independent of the shot's screen position. The guide does not follow parallax offsets or changing scenery layers.

This is a creator-set flat or two-point line, not an automatically detected floor, per-object contact surface or scene collision solver. Curved or changing floors, jumps, perspective, parallax depth, transparent shoe padding and intentionally overlapping artwork can produce misleading warnings. It does not correct the animation or prove a contact or clipping error. The neutral-pose warning remains separate, so moving an entire character down can cross the saved scene guide while staying neutral relative to the rig.

For a picture or vector foot moving sideways near that estimated floor, choose **Hold right/left Foot at Frame N** to move its measured sole horizontally back to the previous sample. This adds one editable foot joint key at that frame, at most 12 stage pixels, and saves through the normal project history. Undo restores the original. The correction requires an existing linear key interval with no timed gesture or talking cycle; it refuses an existing key at that time, automatic motion and unsupported rigs. It keeps the other foot, original endpoints and character path in place, but its interpolation changes the selected foot in nearby frames. Inspect the whole step and its scenery floor before export. It cannot guarantee balance, no clipping, or a sustained planted walk.

Choose a **Foot hold length** of 2–6 frames (three by default), then **Hold right/left Foot for N Frames from Frame N** for a brief planted pose. This works on a vector boot or an uploaded picture foot. It inserts one editable foot joint key per selected 24-fps sample as a single Undo step, holding the original measured sole X. The option is unavailable if too few review samples remain. The entire span must stay within 12 stage pixels of the uncorrected sole; the tool refuses authored keys or unsupported motion anywhere in it. The original endpoint keys, actor path, other foot and picture stay intact. The foot still blends into and out of that short hold, so preview the neighboring frames. It does not solve an entire walking cycle or detect collisions with the scenery.

The focused browser tests position and mirror the picture-sole mark, save and reopen the cartoon, switch reviewed feet, jump to a real frame, review a below-neutral pose, save flat and sloped scene guides, track them with the editor camera, apply painted and vector-foot corrections, and verify backup and Undo/Redo on desktop, phone and tablet. Short-media checks independently decode corrected painted shoes, a vector boot, deliberately lowered and dipping shoes, and a camera-panned painted shoe in exported WebMs. These are technical fixtures to verify measurements and local corrections, not finished cartoon scenes. The existing full-episode technical export remains a separate CI gate; a completed 15–20-minute children's episode still requires original art, voices, editing and artistic review.
